## What it is

A terminal tool for 8BitDo controllers and keyboards on Linux and macOS. It shows what each device supports, and won't write to one unless that's known to be safe.

It is unofficial and not affiliated with 8BitDo.

## What it does today

- Detects connected 8BitDo devices and gives each a plain verdict: working, limited, can't connect, or no access.
- Says for every device what you can do with it now, and what you can't do yet and why.
- **Retro 108 keyboard:** assign any of its 111 keys and buttons to a key with an optional modifier, a media key or a mouse action.
- **Ultimate 2 controller:** a full profile editor runs against a simulated controller. On a real controller it stays blocked until the button map is confirmed on hardware.
- Firmware updates are switched off for now.

## How it was made

AI models were used to assist in development. The Go rewrite was completed by a team of four agents, and I did my due diligence on its security. The README says the same.

## Getting it

Homebrew (`brew tap bybrooklyn/openbitdo && brew install openbitdo`), the AUR (`paru -S openbitdo-bin`), or a release tarball. `openbitdo --mock` previews the interface without any hardware attached.

Mote is about 1,200 lines of TypeScript with no dependencies. It exists so that each small thing I make for this site is the thing itself and not the same loop, canvas and mouse code written again.

<DropBox />

## What it does

- **A stage.** A canvas that fits its box and stays sharp, a loop that moves in fixed steps of 1/120 of a second, the pointer, a few keys, and the page's own colors. It only runs while you can see it, and stops when nothing is moving.
- **2D physics.** Balls and convex shapes with weight, friction and bounce. Pins and ropes between them. You can pick a body up and throw it. Things that come to rest go to sleep.
- **3D on a flat canvas.** Solids made of flat faces, turned and shaded, with no WebGL. Dice that fall and tumble as real solids.
- **A motor.** One body driven toward a place beside another, pushing back as hard: an arm holding a tool.
- **Tests.** Friction angles, toppling, a pendulum's period, a chain that holds, dice that land fairly.

## What runs on it

Everything on the [minis](/minis/) page: a pile of blocks, a chain, a game about rolling a ball along a beam, a climb with a hammer, a solid you can spin, and a dice tower with a D&D helper under it.

## Using it

It is free, under the MIT license. The folder is self-contained, so copy it. A mini is one function that sets a scene up and returns how to step it and how to draw it.

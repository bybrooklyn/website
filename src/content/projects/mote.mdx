Mote is the small engine under every mini on this site. It exists so that each small thing I make is the thing itself and not the same loop, canvas and mouse code written again.

<DropBox />

## What it does

- **A stage.** A canvas that fits its box and stays sharp, a loop that moves in fixed steps of 1/120 of a second, the mouse, a few keys, and the page's own colors. It only runs while you can see it, and stops when nothing is moving. It can hold the mouse inside the box for a game that is all mouse.
- **2D physics.** Boxes, balls and shapes with weight, friction and bounce; walls; pins, rods and ropes; a motor; a hand to pick things up and throw them.
- **3D.** Solids that fall, tumble and hit each other corner to corner, drawn with depth, light and shadow.
- **Tests.** A pile that does not move once it has settled, friction that holds to within a degree, a chain that keeps its length, dice that land fairly.

## What it stands on

Nothing. It is about 2,500 lines of TypeScript and has no dependencies.

For one night it stood on three libraries copied into its source: planck for 2D, cannon for 3D and three for drawing. They did not fix what was wrong with the physics, and the dice tower downloaded 540 KB to roll a die. I took them out. The 2D physics, the 3D physics and the 3D drawing are Mote's own now, and the dice tower downloads 40 KB.

The physics follows the soft step method [Erin Catto described for Box2D 3](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/02/solver2d/). Where things touch is found once a step. The step is then taken in a few small parts, and in each part every contact is solved as a very stiff spring.

## What runs on it

The two things on the [minis](/minis/) page: a pile of blocks, and a dice tower.

## Using it

It is free, under the MIT license. The folder is self-contained, so copy it. A mini is one function that sets a scene up and returns how to step it and how to draw it.

Mote is the small engine under every mini on this site. It exists so that each small thing I make is the thing itself and not the same loop, canvas and mouse code written again.

<DropBox />

## What it does

- **A stage.** A canvas that fits its box and stays sharp, a loop that moves in fixed steps of 1/120 of a second, the mouse, a few keys, and the page's own colors. It only runs while you can see it, and stops when nothing is moving. It can hold the mouse inside the box for a game that is all mouse.
- **2D physics.** Boxes, balls and shapes with weight, friction and bounce; walls; pins, rods and ropes; a motor; a hand to pick things up and throw them.
- **3D.** Solids that fall, tumble and hit each other, drawn with depth, light and shadow.
- **Tests.** A pile that stays still, a chain that holds, an arm that lifts, dice that land fairly.

## What it stands on

Three libraries, forked into Mote's own source and changed there as it needs: planck (a port of Box2D) for 2D, cannon for 3D bodies, and three for drawing in 3D. The first change was to cannon, whose friction held a hundred times harder than the number given: nothing would slide down a ramp. Each keeps its license, and the changes are written down beside them.

## What runs on it

Everything on the [minis](/minis/) page: a pile of blocks, a chain, a game about rolling a ball along a beam, a climb with a hammer, a solid you can spin, and a dice tower with a D&D helper under it.

## Using it

It is free, under the MIT license, as are the three libraries inside it. The folder is self-contained, so copy it. A mini is one function that sets a scene up and returns how to step it and how to draw it.

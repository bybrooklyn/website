## What it is

You get an empty room and a catalog of a few hundred things. You put them in, join them up, and see what happens.

<Figure src="/assets/playmatter.webp" video="/assets/playmatter.mp4" alt="A car beside a tower of wood, glass, and bricks in Playmatter." width={960} height={488} pixel />

## What you can do

- **Spawn ragdolls** and find out what they survive.
- **Build things** out of wood, glass and brick, and join them with hinges.
- **Wire machines together**: coil guns, Tesla coils, whatever you can make work.
- **Set things on fire**, heat them up, or run a current through them.
- **Mod it.** There is a level editor, and mods are written in Luau.

It is in development for Windows and Linux, and goes out as alpha playtest builds. You can get it on [itch.io](https://bybrooklyn.itch.io/playmatter), and everything else about the game is on [playmatter.io](https://playmatter.io).

## How it's built

Playmatter is written in Rust with Bevy and avian2d.

The simulation runs on its own, without the game around it: physics setup, a hinge solver, people and biology, combat, items, heat, fire and electricity. The game is a layer on top: generated pixel art, the interface, tools, an editor, saves, sound, and a Luau mod host.

## How it's tested

A developer harness plays scripted sessions, runs headless benchmark scenes, and runs gameplay checks. A build has to pass four physics checks before anyone plays it.

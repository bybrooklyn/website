I'm Charlotte. I'm a trans woman and a lesbian, and I make a physics sandbox called Playmatter.

## Where I've lived

I was born in Mississippi. I moved to Buffalo in August 2022, and to New Jersey in October 2025.

Mississippi was hot, and the food was phenomenal. People were very narrow-minded, but the hospitality was good, because everyone is so kind (unless you're a minority).

My parent had a job offer in Buffalo, went there for a couple of months and fell in love with the area, so we moved. It wasn't my choice, though I loved it there, and I lived there for three years.

At school I was widely known and outspoken about my beliefs, and people generally liked me (apart from most teenage boys). Most of my friends were women, and still are. I hated being a man throughout, and refused the strongly masculine activities apart from the ones I had to do.

## How I started programming

I started with Scratch, where I made a physics engine, mods for Getting Over It (custom hammers and cheats) and various games of my own. Then I installed Debian on a locked school Chromebook.

A mix of Scratch and my best friend's dad, who is a software engineer, taught me Python. After that I learned more languages, and leaned into AI agents to help me program.

I started homeschooling in 11th grade, and I'm currently enrolled at Mercer County Community College through New Jersey's Jump Start program.

## What being transgender means to me

- My womanhood is an important part of who I am.
- My journey hasn't been completely linear.
- Questioning my identity doesn't invalidate it.
- I want to feel comfortable in my own body.
- I want to experience femininity in my own way.
- I want to be loved and recognized as a woman.
- Being a lesbian is an important part of my identity.
- I want to live authentically without having to constantly justify my existence.

## Why I volunteer

I volunteer at the Pride Center of New Jersey, in youth peer support and on the welcome committee.

I found them by accident, online. I went there and fell in love so hard, because everyone, and I mean everyone, is kind and will talk to you.

What it gave me is happiness. Most of my current friends I met there (being homeschooled makes making friends very, very difficult).

## If you're a queer kid

Find a group of people you can be you around, and find a thing that makes you happy. For me that's programming and the novelty of building software.

It starts knowing nothing. Its legs keep a rhythm, and its brain shapes how each leg swings to it. It is scored on how well it meets its goal, and on how it moves: upright, feet taking turns, not sliding or twitching.

It can learn a generation at a time. Each generation is thirty-two brains, given the same body on the same ground. The best four go on unchanged, and the rest are their children, mixed and changed a little at random. Or it can learn one walker at a time, live: each attempt is a child of the best so far, and is kept only if it does better. You see every attempt.

A run goes a set distance, from two metres to a hundred, or is endless. On a set distance the walker on the screen is the best brain so far, playing back the very run it was scored on, and the rest of its generation are the small marks along the ground. On endless it keeps walking while it stays up, on ground made ahead of it as it goes, and a better brain takes over at the start of a stride.

It can have one leg, two or four. It can be rewarded for going far, going fast, standing tall, jumping, getting along an obstacle course that turns from level ground to hills, steps and gaps, each harder than the last, or carrying a parcel on its back without dropping it. Its brain can be small, medium or large, or a mix of its own, with up to four layers and a little memory that it feeds back to itself from one moment to the next. The brain at the side lights up with what passes through it as it walks. Changing any of these starts the learning again from nothing.

All of it runs in your browser. The body and its physics are written in Rust and run as WebAssembly. "Copy link to this walker" puts its brain in the link, so whoever opens it gets the same walker, already trained.

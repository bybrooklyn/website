It starts knowing nothing. Its legs keep a rhythm, and its brain shapes how each leg swings to it. Each generation is thirty-two brains, and each one is given the same body on the same ground and scored on how well it meets its goal, and on how it moves: upright, feet taking turns, not sliding or twitching. The best four go on to the next generation unchanged, and the rest are their children, mixed and changed a little at random.

The walker on the screen is the best brain so far, playing back the very run it was scored on, from the start each time. When a better one turns up it takes over at the next start, and the flag marks how far it got. The rest of its generation are the small marks along the ground, one for each, where each one got to.

It can have one leg, two or four. It can be rewarded for going far, going fast, standing tall, or jumping, which is best tried on the ground with gaps or steps. Its brain can be bigger, with more neurons in a layer or a second layer; the brain at the side lights up with what passes through it as it walks. Changing any of these starts the learning again from nothing.

All of it runs in your browser. The body and its physics are written in Rust and run as WebAssembly, and the learning goes on in the background while the page draws. "Copy link to this walker" puts its brain in the link, so whoever opens it gets the same walker, already trained.

The dice are real solids. They are dropped in at the top with a spin, hit the ramps and each other, tumble and roll out into the tray, and the number is whichever face ends up on top. The tests roll each kind of die hundreds of times and check that every face comes up as often as chance says it should.

When they have come to rest the camera rises over the tray. The number each die landed on lights up, lifts off the die and joins a row, sorted by kind of die and then by number, with the total under it. A four-sided die has no face on top, so it is read from the top corner, like a real one.

A d100 is two ten-sided dice, tens and ones, and counts as one number. A roll can be up to sixty dice. The last thirty rolls are kept in this browser. "Copy link to this roll" gives a link that makes the same roll again for whoever opens it, with their own luck.

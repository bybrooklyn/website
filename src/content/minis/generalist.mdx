A world is made from a seed, a word or a number, and how hard it is. It can have ground with gaps, ledges, drops, stairs and floating platforms; water and spikes; boxes to push and climb; and platforms that move and see-saws. Each can be switched off, or turned up or down. The seed and the rest are in the address, so a world is a link.

The creature is the Walker's, with two legs or four. It sees with sixteen rays from its eye, each telling it how near the first thing is and what kind of thing it is. "Show what it sees" draws them.

It learns in one of two ways. The page opens on practice. The creature starts knowing nothing, tries, and its brain is changed a little after every batch of runs, each run in a new world of the kind you set. Touching water or spikes, or falling into a gap, costs and ends the run. What it has learned is kept in this browser and picked up on your next visit. "Start over" forgets it.

The other way is evolution, as the Walker does: thirty-two brains a generation, each given the same world from the start, scored by how far it gets. Under evolution it can also hop. On the same amount of computing, practice gets three to six times as far through worlds it has never seen.

All of it runs in your browser. The world, the body and its physics are written in Rust and run as WebAssembly.

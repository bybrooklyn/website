A world is made from a seed, a word or a number, and how hard it is. It can have ground with gaps, ledges, drops, stairs and floating platforms; water and spikes; boxes to push and climb; and platforms that move and see-saws. Each can be switched off, or turned up or down. The seed and the rest are in the address, so a world is a link.

The creature is the Walker's, with two legs or four, and it can hop. It sees with sixteen rays from its eye, each telling it how near the first thing is and what kind of thing it is. "Show what it sees" draws them.

It learns by evolution, as the Walker does: thirty-two brains a generation, each given the same world from the start, scored by how far it gets. Touching water or spikes, or falling into a gap, costs and ends the run. The one on the screen is the best brain so far, playing back the run it was scored on.

This is a first step. Evolution gets it over the easy parts of a world and not much further; learning that does better comes next.

All of it runs in your browser. The world, the body and its physics are written in Rust and run as WebAssembly.

# The Generalist: the plan

Dated 13 November 2026. Everything decided for the Generalist is in [generalist](generalist.md) under
"Decided, not yet built". This file puts it in an order to build in, a stage at a time. The owner has
agreed this order. The stages run one after another without waiting for her; she watches when she likes
and the course changes if she says so. Whether the strict walking test stays the bar for shipping is
decided after stage 2.

## Where it stands

Built: a body, sixteen rays of sight, a plain network of two layers of 64 neurons, practice in the
browser, generated worlds. Two legs keep a foot down and take turns after about two minutes.

What is wrong with it, as the owner put it: it does not feel real or lifelike; it is not clear why it moves
as it does; the brain is small; most of what was discussed is not built; learning is poor; touching the
world feels wrong and breaks things. Picking it up, pushing things, and its taking no notice of the person
are the three named.

Why: the effort went into passing a walking test. A small network trained for two minutes to avoid costs
moves like something avoiding costs. It has nothing it wants, nothing it notices and nothing it remembers.

## How each stage is judged

By watching first: would a visitor say it is alive, and can they see why it did what it did. Then by
numbers kept from the start, for the article. A stage is done when both hold in the real page in a
browser, across several seeds and worlds, and nothing built before is worse. Nothing cruel is done to the
creature. Nothing trained is shipped, except the small vision encoder already agreed. Everything is written
from scratch in the Rust engine and the site's own TypeScript. The Walker's results never change.

## The stages

### 1. Touching it works, and it notices

The foundation, since everything after is shown by touching the world.

- Picking it up: held gently by the body, it hangs, kicks, and lands on its feet or falls as it would; no
  fling, no tangle, and learning goes on from where it was.
- Pushing things: boxes and platforms move as heavy things do when dragged, never through anything, and
  the creature feels a box pushed into it.
- It notices: touch on the body and feet as senses; being lifted, dropped, pushed and having something
  dropped near it reach the brain, and it is seen to react (a flinch, a look, a step back) without a rule
  saying how.
- Learning does not start over or get worse when the world is touched or a setting changed.

**State (13 November 2026): built, on its branch, to be looked at.** One hand for everything, a spring
with a cap on its force, so the creature hangs and kicks and nothing is flung, tangled or dragged through
anything; boxes lag by their weight, a moving platform taken stays level and where it is let go of.
Touch on the body and feet, being held, and a jolt reach the brain, and two legs practise with a visitor
who lifts, nudges and shoves. Every touch changes what it does; two legs learned to come through a box
dropped or slid into them and a nudge, with no rule written for it. They have not learned to land when
let go from a height or by a foot, and four legs learned nothing from the visitor, so they practise
without one. A new world goes on with what was learned; a new body takes up its own or starts afresh, and
the page says which. Both bodies go as far through worlds as before. The numbers are in
`walker/README.md`, "Touching it".

### 2. It wants things

- Energy that runs down and food to find. Tired, its muscles weaken and it slows; it rests to recover. It
  never dies of hunger.
- Curiosity: a reward for reaching and seeing what it has not before.
- Eyes it moves, sharp in the middle, with a neuron for looking around; the page draws where it looks.
- Play: stretches with no task.
- The page opens on a newborn falling over, with the brain, the senses and the energy drawn live.

### 3. The brain that was discussed

- A larger pool of neurons under a cap, with growth going into connections.
- Neurons with their own memory and timing; rhythm neurons the brain conducts; mood neurons that turn the
  rest up or down.
- Connections that change during a run by a learned rule, shown by changing the world and watching it adapt.
- A few sliders for the visitor (how much memory, how much rhythm, how many connections it may afford).
  Changing the shape keeps what was learned.
- The brain drawn so that a visitor can see it think: which neurons are of which kind, what is firing, what
  just changed.
- Learning made faster and steadier with it: measured against the plain network on the same computing.

### 4. A world that changes while it lives

- The ground (slippery, sticky, bouncy, soft), gravity and wind, day and night, things dropped in front
  of it.
- The teacher: worlds evolved to stay at the edge of what it can do.

### 5. The risky bet

A brain of a fixed number of neurons that adds and cuts connections while it lives, each paid for in
energy from the food it finds, the rule for adding and cutting found by evolution while practice trains
the weights. If it works, neurons whose whole rule is an evolved program. If it does not, stage 3 is the
base and the article says so.

### 6. Doing things, and a second creature

- Using things: push a box to a ledge, ride a platform, press a switch.
- Two creatures: rivals for food and helpers with what one cannot do alone, with no rule about which. A
  voice of a few tones, fainter with distance. The page draws the calls, plays them softly, and writes a
  guess at what a call tends to mean, marked as a guess.

### 7. Keeping and sharing

- A creature saved as a small file and loaded again.
- Culture: each new creature watches the last one's best runs, then practises.
- The graphics card for the brain's arithmetic, measured.
- The small vision encoder and the picture experiment, after the training run is agreed.

### 8. The article

A short story of building it, then a plain technical write-up with the numbers and the commands to rerun
them. Claims are narrow: each says what was searched for, and the rest is known methods rebuilt from
scratch and playable in a tab.

## How it is built

A stage at a time, each by an agent on a branch of its own, held to four of the machine's cores while
other work is going on and given the whole machine at night. Each stage ends with a report of what was
tried and what each thing did, pictures of the page, and the numbers. It is merged when its tests pass and
it has been looked at in the page. The page stays a draft until the owner says it ships.

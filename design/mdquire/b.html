A walk by the river

The path starts where the houses stop. It runs along the water for a mile or so, past the boatyard and the old mill, and then turns up into the trees. I walk it most mornings before the day has made up its mind.

Nothing happens on it, which is the point. A heron stands in the shallows and does not move. The light comes through the leaves in pieces. By the time I reach the bridge I have usually worked out what I meant to say the night before.

What to bring

  • Shoes that do not mind mud.
  • Something warm for the shade under the trees.
  • A notebook, or at least a phone with room in it.

Walking is the best way I know of thinking without trying to.

Some days the river is high and brown and loud, and the path is under water at the low bend. Then I go round by the lane, which is longer and has a dog that barks at everyone.
